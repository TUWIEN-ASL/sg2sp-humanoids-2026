From Demonstrations to Symbolic Planning

Abstract

Robotic demonstrations provide a rich source of task knowledge. Explicit symbolic representations offer a structured interface between robotic observations and symbolic planning, enabling task knowledge to be reused across different tasks. However, constructing planning-ready symbolic representations from robotic demonstrations remains challenging, particularly in achieving temporally consistent abstraction and accurately grounded symbolic states.

We propose a framework that converts robotic demonstrations into planning-ready symbolic representations by first segmenting demonstrations into symbolically consistent segments and then generating a symbolic scene graph for each segment through robot-prior-informed reasoning. The resulting scene graphs are further lifted into reusable PDDL operators for downstream symbolic planning. Experiments on a multi-task manipulation dataset show that our method produces more accurate and planning-ready scene graphs than existing zero-shot baselines. We further demonstrate a pipeline that reuses the automatically lifted symbolic operators for planning on previously unseen real-world tasks.
