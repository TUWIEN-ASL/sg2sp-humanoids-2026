From Demonstrations to Symbolic Planning:
Robotic Scene Graph Generation via Symbolic-Consistent Temporal Abstraction

Accepted to IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids 2026)

Haoyi Zheng1, Daniel Sliwowski1, Dongheui Lee1,2
1 Autonomous Systems Lab, Technische Universität Wien (TU Wien)
2 Institute of Robotics and Mechatronics, German Aerospace Center (DLR)
Project Video Abstract

Abstract

Robotic demonstrations provide a rich source of task knowledge, which could be leveraged for robotic task planning. Explicit symbolic representations offer a structured interface between robotic observations and symbolic planning, enabling task knowledge to be reused across different tasks. However, constructing planning-ready symbolic representations from robotic demonstrations remains challenging, particularly in achieving temporally consistent abstraction and accurately grounded symbolic states.

We propose a framework that converts robotic demonstrations into planning-ready symbolic representations by first segmenting demonstrations into symbolically consistent segments and then generating a symbolic scene graph for each segment through robot-prior-informed reasoning. The resulting scene graphs are further lifted into reusable Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) operators for downstream symbolic planning. Experiments on a multi-task manipulation dataset show that our method produces more accurate and planning-ready scene graphs than existing zero-shot baselines while avoiding excessive VLM inference. We further demonstrate a pipeline that reuses the automatically lifted symbolic operators for planning on previously unseen real-world tasks.

Key Contributions

Symbolically Consistent Temporal Abstraction

Segment robotic demonstrations into temporally consistent intervals to support the construction of planning-ready symbolic states.

Robot-Prior-Informed Scene Graph Generation

Generate a symbolic scene graph for each segment using reasoning informed by robot priors, with the goal of producing accurately grounded symbolic states.

Reusable Symbolic Operators

Lift the resulting scene graphs into PDDL operators for downstream planning and demonstrate reuse on previously unseen real-world tasks.
